Why CME

Not a directory. An advisory.

Most listing platforms are search engines with a commission. We're the opposite: a small, senior team of advisors who know every home we put in front of you — and tell you honestly when we don't think it's the right fit.

Verified, not just listed

Every home on our platform has been inspected, priced, and photographed by an advisor who has stood inside it. No ghost listings, no reused photos, no surprise fees buried in the fine print.

Cross-border, end-to-end

Three countries under one roof. Title searches, foreign-buyer trusts, notary coordination, tax filings — handled by people who do this weekly, not once a year.

Off-market first

Before you ask

Questions buyers actually ask us.

Yes — Ecuador places no restrictions on foreign ownership of residential real estate. Foreign buyers receive the same title protections as citizens, and purchases can be made in your name or through an Ecuadorian trust. Our Guayaquil and Quito teams handle the full process, from title search to notarization.

We coordinate the entire transaction remotely. You'll have a single advisor, a shared document vault, and scheduled video walkthroughs at each stage. Wire transfers, notary appointments, and local filings are all scheduled in your timezone — so you don't need to fly anywhere unless you want to.

Buyers pay nothing to work with us. Our advisory fee is paid by the seller or developer at closing — standard in each market. Off-market and private-placement deals are fee-disclosed upfront, always before you tour.

In the U.S., yes — we have preferred lender relationships across our markets. In Ecuador, we support BIESS financing applications and work with local banks. In Puerto Rico, U.S.-based financing and local lenders are both available.

Many of our clients buy remotely. We conduct live video walkthroughs, arrange third-party inspections, and provide notarized condition reports before any wire is sent. Remote closings are routine for us — not the exception.

Cash purchases close in 21–30 days in the U.S., 30–45 days in Ecuador, and 45–60 days in Puerto Rico. Financed purchases add two to four weeks for underwriting — and we'll tell you upfront if a timeline is unrealistic.
